title: Can We Trust the Quality of Software Produced Collaboratively with Agentic AI? Observations Based on the Development of Two Modeling Tools.
presenter: Professor Daniel Amot, University of Ottawa
date: 2026-11-09
time: 18:00
category: seminars

AI coding agents are reshaping how modeling tools are built, but they raise a question the modeling community has not yet answered systematically: how do we measure, and ultimately trust, a tool that an AI largely wrote? This presentation reports on two case studies in which agentic AI (Claude Code) co-developed enterprise modeling tools, and in which the collaborative development record itself was treated as a measurable artifact.

The first case is PM4Py-UCM, an open-source process-mining tool that discovers executable Use Case Map models from event logs (online demo). Built over ten weeks through 18 agent sessions and over 10,000 tool actions, fixes outnumbered features by 130% and nearly one collaborative interaction in five aimed to correct an agent mistake. Independent quality assessment (test coverage, cyclomatic complexity, security findings) shows that quality was however not sacrificed under rapid agent-driven growth. A major contributor is that a deliberate verification scaffolding was in place: oracle-based checks, metamorphic transforms, and byte-deterministic round-trips against the reference metamodel and editor (jUCMNav).

The second case is SymboleoAC-Web, an online IDE for developing and explaining SymboleoAC contract specifications, and for compiling them into deployable smart contracts (online demo). Through the specification of complex contractual terms from a standard (Incoterms), the agent operated this time at the language level and at themeta level, not only to drive the generation of specifications from contractual text, but also to co-evolve the specification language itself and its tool ecosystem to better cover parts of the contracts that could not be specified properly.  Each validation layer (compilation, executable tests, on-chain deployment of the resulting smart contracts) caught a defect class the previous layer could not.

Across both cases, three measurement themes emerge: process quality (classifying human-agent interaction to understand where agents help and where they fail); product quality (independent metrics providing a trust argument beyond the agent's own and at time biased assurances); and semantic quality (why silent corruption in model transformations is a hard problem, and how executable oracles convert invisible failures into visible ones). Practical recommendations for quality-conscious, human-in-the-loop agentic development of modeling tooling will be shared.


#### Biography

Daniel Amyot is Professor in the School of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science (EECS) and the School of Engineering Design and Teaching Innovation (SEDTI), both at the University of Ottawa, Canada. He is also the Director of the Online Master's degree in Digital Transformation and Innovation. From 2002 to 2013, he was Associate Rapporteur on Formal languages and telecommunication software at the International Telecommunication Union (ITU-T), where he led the development of the User Requirements Notation (URN) standard. His research interests span many domains, from requirements/software engineering to modelling (scenarios, goals, and business processes), regulatory compliance/intelligence, process mining, legal/smart contracts, and healthcare informatics. He now leads the development of the Symboleo/SymboleoAC language and its related tools. Daniel holds a Ph.D. and an M.Sc. from the University of Ottawa (2001 and 1994), as well as a B.Sc. from Laval University (1992), all in Computer Science. He is Professional Engineer (OIQ), Senior Member of IEEE, I.S.P (CIPS), ACM, and former Chair of the SDL Forum Society (2019-2022) and of the Steering Committee of the Requirements Engineering conference (2019-2021).
